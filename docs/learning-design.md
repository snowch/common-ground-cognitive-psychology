# Learning design: cognitive psychology

## Brief and audience

The learner wants to understand everyday behavior using academically recognizable concepts and proportionate evidence, rather than diagnose people or memorize attractive labels. This course serves curious adult beginners who choose to learn at their own pace. No psychology prerequisite, account, LMS, certificate or remote response collection is required.

The user authorized using the existing Common Ground social psychology course as the template, including its narrated, responsive, offline format. Its learning and design conventions are retained while the curriculum, transfer cases and concept figures are newly authored. The learning and frontend skills credited in README were read for this work.

## Performance goals and sequence

| Chapter | Focus | Observable goal |
|---------|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | Studying cognition | Explain how a comparison can distinguish cognitive explanations, and identify one limit of a behavioral measure. |
| 2 | Perception and expectations | Distinguish sensory input from perceptual interpretation, and explain how context and uncertainty can affect a judgment. |
| 3 | Attention and cognitive control | Explain a missed event using task demands, and propose a comparison that separates attention from signal visibility. |
| 4 | Working memory | Identify a working-memory demand and redesign instructions without assuming one fixed capacity for everyone. |
| 5 | Long-term memory and learning | Choose a study method that tests delayed recall, and distinguish immediate ease from durable learning. |
| 6 | Reconstructive memory | Separate an event record from later information, and design a less leading question about a remembered event. |
| 7 | Language and understanding | Identify ambiguity and use a specific comprehension check without treating agreement as proof of understanding. |
| 8 | Reasoning and problem solving | Distinguish confirming evidence from a discriminating test, and reframe a problem without assuming a single strategy fits every task. |
| 9 | Judgment under uncertainty | Use a relevant base rate and absolute outcomes to examine a judgment without treating a shortcut as automatically irrational. |
| 10 | Metacognition and integration | Evaluate a confidence judgment against performance, and design a check that combines evidence, retrieval and uncertainty. |

Each chapter contains nine independently navigable pages: fictional story, first decision, three focused explanations, evidence check, new transfer decision with written reasoning, retrieval questions and a field exercise. Distinct explanations have their own page and narration. Forty research disclosures add optional detail; essential limitations stay in the main text. Two decisions per chapter provide explanatory feedback for each of three options. The final written case draws across all ten chapters and includes suggested reasoning and a rubric.

## Presentation plan

Retain the requested Common Ground identity: paper #EDF3F8, ink #19394D, ocean #216B88, muted #4B6172, plum #7058A3 and brass #D8AC5C. Libre Baskerville carries headings; Arimo carries body text and controls. Left-aligned text, short paragraphs and clear evidence disclosures support reading. Desktop pairs text and the primary figure; smaller screens put controls between text and figure. Browser offline access is the primary way to save the course, with chapter files as a secondary option.

Each concept figure has a semantic plan in `visual-models.json`, an accessible description and a caption stating its scope. Process arrows indicate a sequence; branching accounts and conceptual distributions are qualified. Illustrative counts and curves are explicitly identified. The course does not require learners to open an alternative visual to encounter an essential idea.

## Evidence and evaluation

OpenStax provides accessible introductory reading. Named original studies, reviews and theoretical publications support deeper study. DOI titles were checked against Crossref; the open reading pages returned HTTP 200. `source-check.json` records those bounded checks. The text paraphrases ideas in original wording and does not bundle third-party prose or figures. Source selection is not a systematic review.

Validation includes curriculum-to-assessment alignment, all 90 pages at five widths, expanded research and quiz accessibility states, all feedback variants, private writing restore, figure bounds, print overflow, audio metadata and playback, and real browser offline use. Full transcription screening and phoneme inspection are automated checks, not human listening approval.

There has been no independent academic review or learner pilot. Page visits are not mastery scores. The delayed new case tests the intended reasoning more directly than recognition or confidence, but does not demonstrate every real-world transfer or a broad improvement in cognitive ability.

Shared-origin checks confirm that each course keeps separate choices, written responses, offline caches and progress messages. Removing one saved course preserves the other. The inherited offline player was tightened to filter messages by course scope.
