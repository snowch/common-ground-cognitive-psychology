# Cognitive Psychology learner-facing review

The learner knows everyday language and can use a browser. The course assumes no psychology background. I applied the wording and layout rubric first to the published `a53d28a` course and again to the revised course. The review included the home and course guidance, 90 lesson pages in ten chapters, all 60 decision feedback variants, 40 research notes and figures, the glossary, the final case, the online companions, and the offline and chapter-download pages. It covered directions, headings, visible labels, examples, answers, and status and failure messages. The narration scripts were included in the reading pass.

| Rubric | Opening finding | Closing check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepared state, action and result | The first decision asked for an explanation but supplied only choice controls. Transfer said “Compare reasoning” even though the control only reveals feedback for the selected option. Recall instructions stayed the same after the reveal. | Decisions name the actual **Check choice** control and explain which evidence it shows. A first-decision link opens its story. Transfer names the editable writing area and the comparison learners make themselves. After checking or revealing, instructions direct learners to the explanation on screen. Field exercises and the final case name paper or private notes as their writing location. |
| Terms, evidence and scope | Essential explanations for concepts appeared below the fold in a transcript that was always open. The phrase “cautious volunteer” could imply either reporting less or trying to miss fewer warnings. The recall question about percentage points lacked its starting and ending rates on that page. | All 30 main concept pages show the recorded explanation and the short terms and cues beside the figure. The signal-detection page explains the volunteer’s goal, reporting threshold and possible changes in both hits and false alarms. The recall question states four to two failures per hundred. Fictional scenes, teaching counts and observed research retain their distinct evidence claims. All 160 recordings validate against their scripts and hashes. |
| Progression and optional material | Play and Next competed as primary controls. The open transcript repeated visible exercises. Some optional research panels duplicated main text or the same limit. | Each stage has one primary control for progressing, checking or revealing. Feedback and answers follow the control and receive keyboard focus. Exact exercise transcripts, research, glossary and supplemental figures have named disclosures. Fifty repeated research passages were removed after comparison with the main text. The study companion reveals suggested reasoning and self-assessment criteria separately. |
| Feedback, storage and access | A failed write left generic storage wording. An offline update offered two equally prominent actions. | Failed storage leaves the editing field usable and explains where the current tab’s changes reside. A later successful write clears that failure message. Existing choices, checks, responses and progress use the original storage format. Visit counts measure navigation. The offline page names the save and reopen actions, with a single primary action in each state. |

The QA walkthrough used Chromium to follow keyboard decisions, recall and glossary controls, all 60 feedback variants, and the mobile and desktop layouts across all 90 lessons at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 pixels. The existing light palette was checked under both system colour preferences. The original published profile supplied previously checked and unchecked decisions, two written responses and visit history for the restore check. Independent storage-failure simulation exercised copying and later saving a tab-only draft. The offline walkthrough saved a full copy, reopened and played lessons without a network, and removed the downloaded files while retaining progress. The combined deck remains 90 pages. The revised chapter 9 and study guide PDFs were checked in print.

These tests verify course controls, representative playback, layout, storage and preservation of the original recordings. They do not measure learner understanding or teaching effectiveness. The workflow now runs the content, recording and hosted-build checks on pull requests before the main-branch Pages workflow publishes.
